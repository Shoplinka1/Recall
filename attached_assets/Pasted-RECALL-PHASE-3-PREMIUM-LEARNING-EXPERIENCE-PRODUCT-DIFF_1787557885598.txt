1. PREMIUM LANDING PAGE

Rewrite ALL landing-page copy.

Do not use generic AI/SaaS phrases such as:
"Revolutionize your learning"
"Unlock your potential"
"AI-powered learning platform"
"Study smarter, not harder"

Instead communicate a clear product promise.

Core positioning:

Recall turns a student's own study material into focused active-recall practice, identifies what they actually remember, teaches weak concepts, and brings them back to the concepts they need to strengthen.

The landing page should immediately answer:

WHAT IS RECALL?
WHO IS IT FOR?
WHY IS IT BETTER THAN JUST REREADING?
HOW DOES IT WORK?
WHY SHOULD I TRUST IT?
WHY SHOULD I SIGN UP?

Create premium sections such as:

HERO
- strong concise headline
- supporting explanation
- primary CTA
- secondary CTA if appropriate
- polished visual treatment

HOW IT WORKS
1. Add your study material
2. Recall turns it into focused questions
3. Answer and receive useful feedback
4. Recall identifies what needs strengthening
5. Return to weak concepts until they stick

WHY RECALL FEELS DIFFERENT
Explain the product's actual differentiators:
- questions grounded in the learner's own material
- active recall instead of passive rereading
- concept-level mastery
- targeted teaching after mistakes
- meaningful follow-up questions
- persistent progress
- adaptive reinforcement

Include a concise example showing:

SOURCE MATERIAL
→ QUESTION
→ ANSWER
→ FEEDBACK
→ TEACHING
→ FOLLOW-UP
→ MASTERY

Use real product behavior, not fake claims.

PREMIUM TRUST SECTION

Communicate that Recall:
- works from the student's own material
- does not invent study facts
- keeps learning progress persistent
- focuses on understanding rather than meaningless scores

CTA SECTION

End with a strong reason to start studying.

2. PREMIUM WRITING / COPY

Review every visible piece of product copy.

Improve:
- buttons
- headings
- subtitles
- empty states
- onboarding text
- loading messages
- error messages
- answer feedback
- results copy
- progress labels
- study instructions

Copy should be concise, human, confident and useful.

Avoid robotic AI wording.

Examples of desired tone:

Instead of:
"Submit your answer to continue."

Prefer:
"Trust your recall."

Instead of:
"Incorrect answer."

Prefer:
"Not quite. Let's lock this concept in."

Instead of:
"Correct answer."

Prefer:
"Exactly. You remembered it."

Do not overdo motivational language. Keep it sophisticated.

3. PREMIUM STUDY EXPERIENCE

Make the question screen feel exceptional on mobile.

Each question should have:
- clear hierarchy
- minimal distraction
- obvious answer area
- confidence selection where supported
- clear submit/check action
- elegant correct/incorrect feedback
- concise explanation
- source grounding where appropriate
- teaching intervention when triggered
- follow-up question when available

The student should always understand:

"What am I answering?"
"How confident am I?"
"Was I right?"
"Why?"
"What should I remember?"
"What happens next?"

4. MAKE RECALL FEEL ADAPTIVE

The product should communicate that Recall is paying attention to the learner.

Use the existing mastery/weakness data to make the experience feel personalized.

Examples:

"You're getting stronger on Photosynthesis."

"This concept needs another pass."

"Let's reinforce this one."

"Strong recall. Keep going."

Do NOT invent mastery values.

Use only persisted real learner data.

5. RESULTS EXPERIENCE

Make results feel like a useful learning diagnosis, NOT just a score screen.

Prioritize:

WHAT YOU KNOW
WHAT NEEDS WORK
WHAT YOU SHOULD REVIEW NEXT

Show meaningful concept-level information using existing mastery data.

Avoid making the experience feel like a generic exam result.

The results screen should naturally lead into:

"Study again"
"Strengthen weak concepts"
"Continue learning"

without creating fake data or changing backend scoring.

6. MICRO-INTERACTIONS

Add subtle premium motion where appropriate:

- question transitions
- answer feedback
- progress updates
- mastery changes
- result reveal
- loading states

Keep animations:
- fast
- subtle
- purposeful

No excessive bouncing, flashy effects, or distracting animations.

9. EMPTY / LOADING / ERROR STATES

Every important state should feel intentional.

Examples:

Loading:
"Building your recall session..."

Saving:
"Saving your answer..."

Connection failure:
"Your answer wasn't saved. Check your connection and try again."

Empty study material:
"Add something you're studying and we'll turn it into recall practice."

Do not hide real errors.

→ ACTIVE RECALL
